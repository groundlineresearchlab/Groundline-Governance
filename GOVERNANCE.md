## 1. Purpose

This document defines the public governance framework of Groundline Research Lab.

It establishes the visible authority structure, functional boundaries, repository relationships, research integrity principles, publication boundaries, and continuity requirements used across Groundline Research Lab.

This document does not disclose internal Control Room operations, private decision processes, restricted research methods, credentials, security information, or confidential operational material.

## 2. Groundline Research Lab Function

Groundline Research Lab is the evidence and research layer within the wider MaMeeFarm™ ecosystem.

Its core functional identity is:

**Data • Evidence • Provenance • Research Systems**

Groundline exists to investigate how evidence is created, structured, connected, evaluated, preserved, and understood.

Research begins with questions, observation, and available evidence rather than predetermined conclusions.

## 3. Governance Principles

Groundline Research Lab operates according to the following principles:

1. Evidence before interpretation.
2. Provenance before reuse.
3. Observation before conclusion.
4. Traceability before simplification.
5. Qualification before certainty.
6. Public and private material must remain clearly separated.
7. Historical research states must remain traceable.
8. Material corrections must be documented.
9. Repository function must remain distinguishable from repository ownership.
10. Research conclusions must remain within the available evidentiary scope.

## 6. Research and Publication Separation

Research activity and public publication are separate governance states.

Research repositories may contain:

- Working research
- Evidence development
- Source examination
- Research notes
- Methodological work
- Unresolved questions
- Internal synthesis

Public repositories may contain only material approved for public release.

Public availability must not be treated as proof that all underlying research material is public.

## 7. Evidence Discipline

Research claims should remain connected to identifiable evidence where evidence is available.

Evidence may include:

- Primary documents
- Official records
- Institutional publications
- Research papers
- Structured datasets
- Archived records
- Direct observations
- Reproducible calculations
- Clearly identified secondary sources

The presence of a source does not automatically validate a claim.

Source authority, scope, date, methodology, context, and limitations must be considered separately.

## 8. Research State Separation

Groundline should preserve distinctions among:

**Observation:** What was directly found, measured, recorded, or reported.

**Evidence:** Material supporting or constraining an observation.

**Structure:** Relationships, classifications, processes, or system organization identified from evidence.

**Interpretation:** Assessment of what the evidence may mean.

**Hypothesis:** A proposition requiring further evidence.

**Conclusion:** A finding supported within a defined evidentiary scope.

These states must not be collapsed into one another without explanation.

## 9. Provenance

Groundline research should preserve sufficient provenance to determine:

1. Where information originated.
2. When it was retrieved or observed.
3. Which record used it.
4. Whether it is primary, secondary, derived, or interpreted.
5. Whether transformations were applied.
6. Whether later records corrected, expanded, or superseded earlier work.

Provenance supports verification.

Provenance alone does not establish factual correctness.

## 10. Historical Integrity

Groundline preserves research and repository history.

Canonical historical records should not be silently rewritten merely because later evidence, governance, interpretation, or architecture changes.

Where material correction is required, preferred methods include:

- Correction record
- Revision note
- Superseding record
- Forward governance update
- Traceable commit

Earlier states remain part of the development history.

The absence of internal information from public governance must not be interpreted as absence of internal governance.

## 12. Operational Independence

Individual Groundline repositories may operate under different schedules, production modes, publication rules, and access classifications.

A shared governance framework does not require identical operating procedures.

Repository specific governance applies within the scope of that repository unless superseded by a higher authorized governance rule.

## 16. Governance Changes

Governance evolves forward.

Material governance changes should be documented through repository history.

A new rule should not silently redefine the historical governance state of records created under an earlier rule.

Where appropriate, a governance change should identify:

- What changed
- Effective date
- Scope
- Affected repositories or functions
- Relationship to previous governance
